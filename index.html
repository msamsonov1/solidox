Solid Oxide Electrolysis Physics & Dynamic Inversion Theory

NASA’s Mars Oxygen ISRU Experiment (MOXIE) proved solid oxide electrolysis on another world. 1. Electrolysis Kinetics & Nernst Overpotential

The cell operating potential $V_{\text{cell}}$ is determined by the open-circuit Nernst potential $E_{\text{Nernst}}$ augmented by ohmic losses in the Yttria-Stabilized Zirconia (YSZ) and activation/concentration overpotentials at the porous nickel cermet cathode:

$$V_{\text{cell}} = E^0(T) + \frac{R_g T}{2F} \ln\left( \frac{P_{\text{CO}} \cdot P_{\text{O}_2}^{1/2}}{P_{\text{CO}_2}} \right) + I \cdot R_{\text{YSZ}}(T) + \eta_{\text{act}} + \eta_{\text{conc}}$$

Where $R_g$ is the universal gas constant, $F = 96485 \text{ C/mol}$, and $R_{\text{YSZ}}(T)$ follows an Arrhenius ionic conduction profile:

$$\sigma_{\text{ionic}}(T) = \frac{\sigma_0}{T} \exp\left( -\frac{E_a}{k_B T} \right) \implies R_{\text{YSZ}} = \frac{L_{\text{membrane}}}{\sigma_{\text{ionic}} \cdot A_{\text{active}}}$$

2. Carbon Coking & Triple-Phase Boundary Deactivation

When the cathode local partial pressure of $\text{CO}$ exceeds thermodynamic equilibrium or when cathodic overpotential $\eta_{\text{cath}}$ drives the carbon activity $a_{\text{C}} > 1$, solid graphite nucleates via the Boudouard disproportionation reaction:

$$2\text{CO}_{(\text{g})} \rightleftharpoons \text{C}_{(\text{s})} + \text{CO}_{2(\text{g})}, \quad \Delta G^{\circ}_{\text{Boudouard}} = -172.5\text{ kJ/mol} + 175.7 \cdot T \text{ J/(mol}\cdot\text{K)}$$

Solid carbon plugs the porous Ni-YSZ Triple Phase Boundary (TPB) length $\lambda_{\text{TPB}}$, reducing electrocatalytic active area and causing a severe non-linear divergence in the apparent internal resistance $R$:

$$\frac{dR}{dQ} = \left(\frac{\partial R}{\partial \lambda_{\text{TPB}}}\right)\left(\frac{\partial \lambda_{\text{TPB}}}{\partial m_{\text{soot}}}\right) \cdot \frac{k_{\text{coke}}(a_{\text{C}} - 1)}{I} + \left.\frac{dR}{dQ}\right|_{\text{nominal}}$$
